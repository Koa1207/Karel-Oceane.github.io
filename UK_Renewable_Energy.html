UK Renewable Energy Consumption: 1990-2020


Executive Summary

This dataset provides a detailed overview of the United Kingdom’s energy consumption, covering 17 distinct energy sources alongside total consumption figures.

The data is measured in million tonnes of oil equivalent (mtoe), where 1 mtoe = 42 petajoules (42 × 1015 J). It includes the percentage of total consumption sourced from renewables.

Dataset Overview

  • Sources Covered: 17 energy sources + total
  • Units: Million tonnes of oil equivalent (mtoe)
  • Conversion: 1 mtoe = 42 petajoules
  • Rounding: Values reported to 3 decimal places
  • Adjustments: Cross-boundary correction applied to biofuels

Key Insights

Renewable Energy Consumption Growth

  • 1993–1994: 35% surge in renewable consumption
  • 1996: Minor decline of 2.38%
  • 1997 onwards: Sustained growth with fluctuations

Growth was supported by infrastructure investments, improved renewable technology, and rising urgency around climate change.

Top 5 Renewable Energy Sources in 2020

  1. Wind/Wave/Tidal: Grew from 0.001 mtoe (1990) to 6.481 mtoe (2020)
  2. Wood Energy: Stable contributor ranging from 1.108–3.367 mtoe
  3. Municipal Solid Waste (MSW): Increased from 0.065 to 2.469 mtoe
  4. Biomass: Increased from 0.074 to around 2.1–2.4 mtoe
  5. Biodiesel: Reached 0.359 mtoe in 2020

Recommendations

Policy & Regulatory Support

Implement incentives and reduce barriers for renewable projects. Provide financial support for innovation and scaling.

Public–Private Partnerships

Encourage collaboration between governments, industry, and academia to improve renewable energy development.

Education & Workforce

Build training programs to develop renewable energy skills and prepare workers for future green jobs.

International Collaboration

Share technology, best practices, and strategies globally to support a faster shift to clean energy.

Conclusion

The dataset confirms the UK’s significant progress in renewable energy adoption over the last three decades. Wind, waste-to-energy, and biomass have grown into major contributors.

Continued policy commitment, investment, and international collaboration will be key to sustaining renewable growth.
